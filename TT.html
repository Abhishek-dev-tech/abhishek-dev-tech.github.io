Tempo Travelers

About

Tempo Travelers is a free VR party game on Meta Quest. You guide your Tempo through wild obstacle courses and try to stay ahead of other players.

Play with up to 4 people, bump rivals off the track, and collect coins as you go. The game has different mini-games, like crown chases, team ball battles, and infection modes. You can also unlock skins, trails, and outfits to customize your look.

My Role

I worked on the game's multiplayer systems using Photon Quantum 3, so players could connect and play together smoothly in VR.

I also set up and connected PlayFab for the game. This included Economy v2 (in-game purchases and rewards), analytics (tracking how players use the game), and catalogs (managing items like skins and outfits).

I worked closely with designers, animators, and technical artists to turn ideas into working features and fix issues quickly when art or design needed code support.

On top of gameplay code, I built editor tools inside Unity that helped designers work faster. These tools cut down on repetitive setup work so the team could spend more time on level design and polish.


What I Learned

Working closely with designers, animators, artists, and other programmers taught me how much communication matters on a real game team. When many people are building different parts of the same game, small misunderstandings can turn into big delays. I learned to ask clear questions early, share updates often, and explain technical limits in a way that non-programmers can understand.

I also saw that good communication is not just about talking more — it is about making sure everyone knows the plan, the priorities, and what is blocking progress. That helped features move faster and reduced last-minute fixes before a release.

On the technical side, I learned a lot more about optimization, especially for VR. VR games need to run smoothly at a high frame rate, or players feel sick and stop playing. I spent time finding slow code, reducing unnecessary work each frame, and thinking about performance when building multiplayer and PlayFab features — not only at the end.

Those VR lessons also made me better at optimization in general. I started looking at memory use, network traffic, and how systems scale when more players join. Overall, Tempo Travelers pushed me to write cleaner code, plan better with the team, and always keep performance in mind from the start.
